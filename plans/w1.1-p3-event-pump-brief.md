## Why
The driver already splits in-kernel (P2 reaches ENGAGED without ever reading an event — the spike proved it). The pump is **observability**, not correctness: it drains the kernel event queue on long sessions and gives the ONLY direct proof that "the driver is really splitting app X" (`START/STOP_SPLITTING_PROCESS`) plus early `ERROR_*` signals. Its death must degrade to a flag, never touch the engaged split and never break the net. P3 also runs the **first live acceptance** on windows-brat, validating the whole P2 engage flow end-to-end against real traffic.

## What
`SplitTunnelDriverManager.cs`:
- Fields: `Thread? _pumpThread`, `SafeWaitHandle? _shutdownEvent`, `SafeWaitHandle? _pumpEvent` (the pump's OWN OVERLAPPED event — separate from the control event), one reusable **pinned** `byte[64*1024+64]` buffer + its `GCHandle`, `int _pumpErrorStreak`.
- `StartPumpLocked()` — called at the success tail of `EngageLocked` (after ENGAGED): create shutdown+pump events, start the bg `Thread { IsBackground=true, Name="split-tunnel-events" }`.
- Pump loop (§2.2): issue overlapped `DEQUEUE_EVENT` (out=buffer) → `WaitHandle.WaitAny([pumpEvent, shutdownEvent])` → shutdown ⇒ `CancelIoEx` + drain + exit; completion ⇒ `GetOverlappedResult` → `ParseEventBuffer` (P1) → `ClassifyEvent` (P1) → log at the mapped severity. Errors: `ERROR_OPERATION_ABORTED` ⇒ exit (RESET/cancel); any other ⇒ `++_pumpErrorStreak`; 3 consecutive ⇒ `_pumpHealthy=false` + log degraded + exit. **Never touches `_engaged`.**
- `StopPumpLocked()` — called FIRST in `DisengageLocked` (before RESET, per §2.2 order): signal shutdown, `CancelIoEx`, `Thread.Join(2s)` (not joined ⇒ log + proceed; closing the handle cancels the pended IRP). Free the pinned buffer + events.
- `Dispose`: pump stop folded into `DisengageLocked` already; ensure buffer/events freed.
- `IsPumpHealthy` already wired to `_pumpHealthy` (P2 seam) — now genuinely driven by the loop.
